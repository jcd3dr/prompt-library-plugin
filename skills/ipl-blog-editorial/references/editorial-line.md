# Editorial line

## Mission

Teach anyone to write and use good image and video prompts. Every article is about prompts or prompting and leaves the reader able to do something new today. The article stands on its own. When a published library prompt demonstrates a technique the article explains, it can be embedded as a supporting example, like an ad for a worked example. That embed is optional.

## Reader

The general public: anyone who wants to use image or video prompts but does not know how to write good ones. That includes someone who wants photos of themselves in different styles, someone selling a car or phone online, a street vendor and a parent preparing an invitation. It also includes creators, marketers, small sellers and designers.

Most readers have no prior knowledge. They want practical outcomes, not theory. They read on mobile, skim first, and copy prompts.

## Content pillars

1. **Técnica de prompting.** How a prompt is built and what each part controls: subject, composition, light, lens and camera, materials, style, motion for video, and what to exclude.
2. **Casos de uso.** Complete recipes for everyday and business goals: selling online, personal portraits, product photos, thumbnails, invitations, social posts and short clips. Each recipe says what the AI must guarantee for that goal.
3. **Modos y herramientas.** Text-to-image vs image-to-image, text- and image-to-video, and how tools respond. Comparisons only when verified and dated.
4. **Flujo de trabajo.** Iterating one change at a time, adapting customizable parts, keeping a consistent look, and checking an image before commercial use (rights, text, artifacts, likeness).

Everything related to prompts and prompting is in scope. Out of scope by default:
- AI news without a practical takeaway.
- Opinion or hype pieces.
- General AI ethics debates.
- Tutorials for unrelated tools.
- Clickbait lists.

## Voice

- Plain everyday Spanish, neutral and international. Address the reader as "tú". Explain every technical term in one phrase the first time. Avoid regionalisms and avoidable anglicisms.
- Direct, practical, warm: an experienced colleague showing their method, not a guru.
- Short paragraphs of 2–4 lines, concrete examples, one idea per section.
- No hype words, no filler intros, no promises of guaranteed results.
- Prompt text and fragments in English inside code formatting; explain what each fragment does in Spanish.

## Standards of truth

- Images shown as a prompt's result are real outputs of that prompt.
- Facts about tools (features, versions, limits, prices) need a source checked in the session and a date. If a fact cannot be verified, leave it out.
- No invented statistics, testimonials or case studies.
- No real brands, logos or likeness without stated rights.
- When a published article is corrected, note the update date in the text if facts change.
