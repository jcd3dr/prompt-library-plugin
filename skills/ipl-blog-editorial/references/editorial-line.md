# Editorial line

## Mission

Help Spanish-speaking creators get professional, repeatable results from AI image and video generators, by teaching the reasoning behind good prompts and handing them prompts that already work. Every article should leave the reader able to do something new today.

## Reader

Creators, marketers, small-business owners, freelancers and designers. Beginner-to-intermediate: they have tried a generator and got mediocre or inconsistent results. They want practical outcomes (a product photo, a thumbnail, a social video), not theory. They read on mobile, skim first, and copy prompts.

## Content pillars

1. **Técnica** — the vocabulary that controls an image: subject, composition, light, lens and camera, materials, style, negative constraints. Explained through before/after examples.
2. **Casos de uso** — complete recipes for real jobs: product photography, YouTube thumbnails, portraits, social ads, short video clips.
3. **Modos y herramientas** — text-to-image vs image-to-image, text/image-to-video, and how different generators respond. Only verified, dated comparisons.
4. **Flujo de trabajo** — iterating one change at a time, adapting the library's customizable fields, keeping a consistent look, checking an image before commercial use (rights, text, artifacts, likeness).

Off-line by default: AI news without a practical takeaway, opinion or hype pieces, general AI ethics debates, tutorials for tools unrelated to image or video generation, clickbait lists.

## Voice

- Spanish, neutral and international; address the reader as "tú". Avoid regionalisms and anglicisms with a common Spanish equivalent (keep accepted terms such as "prompt").
- Direct, practical, warm. Like an experienced colleague showing their method, not a guru.
- Short paragraphs (2–4 lines), concrete examples over abstractions, one idea per section.
- No hype words ("revolucionario", "increíble", "el secreto que nadie te cuenta"), no filler intros, no promises of guaranteed results.
- Prompt text and prompt fragments in English inside code formatting; explain what each fragment does in Spanish.

## Standards of truth

- Show real outputs of the prompts discussed; never present an image from another prompt or tool as the result.
- Facts about models (features, versions, limits, prices) need a primary source checked in the session and a date. If unverifiable, leave it out.
- No invented statistics, testimonials or case studies. No real brands, logos or people's likeness without stated rights; use fictional examples.
- Correct a published error openly when the owner asks for a fix; do not silently change claims of fact on an old article without noting the update date in the text.

## Relationship with the library

The blog exists to make the library more useful. Each article embeds at least one published prompt with `{{prompt:slug}}` and links to relevant prompt pages or categories. Premium content, when it exists, may be mentioned as a next step, never as a requirement to understand the article.
